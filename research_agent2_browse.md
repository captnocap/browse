## Executive Summary

Brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) have reached an inflection point in early 2026. What was largely a research curiosity a decade ago is now a rapidly maturing clinical technology with dozens of human participants, multiple FDA-designated breakthrough devices, and billions of dollars in investment. The field is defined by several concurrent trends:

- **Neuralink has implanted 21 participants** across global clinical trials and demonstrated typing speeds up to 40 words per minute through thought alone.
- **Paradromics received FDA approval** in November 2025 for its first long-term clinical trial (Connect-One), targeting speech restoration with a 1,600+ electrode system achieving 200+ bits/second bandwidth.
- **Precision Neuroscience** achieved the first full FDA regulatory clearance for a wireless BCI in April 2025, with its minimally invasive surface-based system implanted in over 50 patients.
- **Columbia University's BISC platform**, published in *Nature Electronics* in December 2025, integrates 65,536 electrodes on a single silicon chip thinner than tissue paper, with 100 Mbps wireless bandwidth -- 100 times faster than any competing wireless BCI.
- **Merge Labs**, backed by $252 million from OpenAI and Sam Altman, launched in January 2026 as an ultrasound-based BCI company aiming to read brain activity non-invasively.
- **UNESCO adopted the first global ethical framework** for neurotechnology in November 2025, establishing safeguards for mental privacy and human dignity.
- **China has declared BCI one of six "industries of the future"** in its 15th Five-Year Plan, with aggressive timelines to integrate BCI into healthcare and manufacturing by 2027.
- The global BCI market is valued at approximately **$3.21 billion in 2025** and is projected to reach **$15.04 billion by 2035** (CAGR of 16.7%).

## Latest Scientific Breakthroughs

### Columbia University's BISC: A New Generation of BCI (December 2025)

Researchers at Columbia University, NewYork-Presbyterian, Stanford University, and the University of Pennsylvania unveiled the **Biological Interface System to Cortex (BISC)** in *Nature Electronics*. This represents a paradigm shift in BCI hardware:

- **Single CMOS chip** thinned to just 50 micrometers (about the thickness of a human hair), occupying approximately 3 mm cubed -- less than 1/1000th the size of conventional BCI devices
- **65,536 electrodes**, 1,024 simultaneous recording channels, and 16,384 stimulation channels
- **100 Mbps wireless data bandwidth** via a custom ultra-wideband radio link -- at least 100 times higher throughput than any competing wireless BCI
- The chip is flexible enough to conform to the brain's surface and slides into the subdural space between the brain and skull through a minimally invasive incision
- Uses TSMC's 0.13-micrometer BCD (Bipolar-CMOS-DMOS) manufacturing technology, enabling mass production
- Human intraoperative recording studies are already underway
- The team has founded **Kampto Neurotech** to commercialize the technology
- An NIH grant has been awarded for implementing BISC in drug-resistant epilepsy management

As lead researcher Ken Shepard stated: "Semiconductor technology has made this possible, allowing the computing power of room-sized computers to now fit in your pocket. We are now doing the same for medical implantables."

### Chinese Academy of Sciences: Real-World Mind-Controlled Wheelchair (December 2025)

The Chinese Academy of Sciences (CAS) Center for Excellence in Brain Science and Intelligence Technology, in collaboration with Huashan Hospital affiliated with Fudan University, achieved a major advance in invasive BCI clinical trials:

- A middle-aged man with quadriplegia from cervical spinal cord injury can now **steer a wheelchair outdoors** and **command a robotic dog to retrieve items** using only his thoughts
- This represents a leap from prior "two-dimensional screen" tasks (cursor control) to **three-dimensional real-world actions**
- Key technical achievements include:
  - Neural data compression and hybrid decoding models that improved brain control system performance by 15-20%
  - "Neural manifold alignment" technique for stability in interpreting fluctuating signals
  - "Online recalibration" technology enabling real-time parameter adjustments during daily activities
  - System delay reduced to **under 100 milliseconds** -- faster than the approximately 200ms it takes a normal person's brain command to translate into physical action
- Ultra-thin electrodes less than 1% the diameter of a human hair were implanted
- A second participant has also been successfully implanted

### NeuroWorm: Dynamic Soft Electrode (September 2025)

Researchers from the Shenzhen Institute of Advanced Technology (CAS) and Donghua University developed **NeuroWorm**, the first "dynamic" soft electrode for BCIs:

- A soft, movable, long-term implantable fiber electrode
- Represents a shift from static to dynamic operation and from passive recording to active, intelligent exploration
- Published in *Nature*

### Merge Labs: Ultrasound-Based Brain Reading (January 2026)

Merge Labs, spun out of non-profit Forest Neurotech, launched with $252 million in funding:

- Co-founded by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, along with researchers Tyson Aflalo, Sumner Norman, and Caltech BCI researcher Mikhail Shapiro
- Uses **functional ultrasound** to image and modulate brain activity
- Less invasive than electrode-based systems -- sensors placed under the skull or through a bone window rather than deep in the brain
- Can monitor large areas of the brain and stimulate multiple sites
- Has demonstrated ability to interpret intended movements of monkeys and detect brain activity behind human actions like playing guitar and video games
- Potential for treating multifaceted disorders such as depression, addiction, and tinnitus
- Limitations include relatively slow temporal resolution since blood flow is an indirect measure of neural activity

### China Tests Wireless BCI in Space (February 2026)

Northwestern Polytechnical University (NPU) in China reportedly completed the **world's first in-orbit verification** of a wireless implantable BCI:

- Device reached orbit in December 2025 via a dedicated space experiment platform
- Maintained stable EEG signal collection in simulated body-fluid environment despite space conditions
- Flexible electrode array that mimics the brain's natural contours
- In animal trials, signal stability improved by several hundredfold compared to standard metal electrodes
- Provides data on how microgravity alters neural firing patterns
- Potential applications in monitoring astronaut cognitive health during long-duration missions

## Major Companies and Their Progress

### Neuralink

**Status:** 21 participants implanted globally as of January 2026

Neuralink remains the most high-profile BCI company and has achieved significant milestones:

- **PRIME Study (US):** Ongoing clinical trial for its Telepathy device. The first patient, Noland Arbaugh, implanted in January 2024, has returned to college to pursue a neuroscience degree using his implant for up to 17 hours daily
- **CAN-PRIME Study (Canada):** Launched November 2024, Neuralink's first international trial
- **GB-PRIME Study (Great Britain):** Launched July 2025 across two UK sites
- **CONVOY Study:** Launched November 2024, a feasibility trial extending BCI control to an investigational assistive robotic arm
- **VOICE Study:** New trial targeting speech restoration, with patients demonstrating typing speeds of up to **40 words per minute** using a ten-finger mental keyboard
- **FDA Breakthrough Device Designations:** Received for both Blindsight (visual restoration, September 2024) and Speech restoration (May 2025)
- **Key participants:**
  - *Noland Arbaugh:* First recipient, uses Telepathy for education, returned to college
  - *Nick:* Controls a robotic arm telepathically, feeding himself and gesturing, achieved over 10 bits per second within his first week
  - *Sebastian:* 23-year-old medical student using Telepathy for up to 17 hours daily
  - *Audrey:* First female Neuralnaut, creates abstract art using thought-controlled cursor, plans to open a physical gallery
  - *Jake:* ALS patient using the mental keyboard for communication
- **Funding:** Raised $650 million Series E in June 2025
- **Production:** Elon Musk announced plans for high-volume production of brain implants
- **12 people had received the chip** as of August 2025, logging more than 15,000 hours of use; grew to 21 by January 2026

### Synchron

**Status:** Transitioning from early feasibility study to pivotal trial

Synchron offers a fundamentally different approach -- a **semi-invasive** BCI that does not require craniotomy:

- The **Stentrode** device is inserted through the jugular vein via catheter and placed near the brain's motor cortex
- First semi-invasive BCI approved by the US FDA for clinical trials
- Approximately 10 people currently use Synchron's BCI
- **COMMAND trial:** Early feasibility study initial phase complete with stable safety record
- **Ecosystem integration strategy:**
  - Connected to **Apple Vision Pro** (May 2025)
  - Integrated with **Amazon Alexa** (September 2024) -- ALS patient controlled smart home with thoughts
  - Launched generative chat feature powered by **OpenAI**
  - Developing Bluetooth protocol for BCIs to auto-detect and connect to iPhones, iPads, Vision Pro
- **Chiral brain foundational model:** Announced at NVIDIA GTC 2025, a generative pre-trained model that learns from neural data
- Collaboration with **NVIDIA Holoscan platform** (January 2025) for next-generation real-time neural processing
- Planning upgraded BCI pilot study on path to pivotal trial

### Paradromics

**Status:** FDA-approved clinical trial launching early 2026

- Received **FDA IDE (Investigational Device Exemption) approval** in November 2025 for the **Connect-One Clinical Study**
- The **Connexus BCI** features 1,600+ electrodes achieving **200+ bits/second bandwidth**
- Will implant two people who lost the ability to speak due to neurological diseases or injuries
- Goals: ensure device safety and restore real-time speech communication
- Previously demonstrated successful decoding of brain activity in a human patient
- Positioned as one of the closest rivals to Neuralink in the invasive BCI space

### Precision Neuroscience

**Status:** First fully FDA-cleared wireless BCI (April 2025)

- Ultra-thin electrode array that sits on the brain's surface rather than penetrating tissue
- **4,000+ electrodes** covering 8 cm squared of brain area
- Implanted in **over 50 patients** with FDA clearance for up to 30-day use
- Participants can type, play games, and control robotic devices through thought
- Co-founded by Dr. Benjamin Rapoport, formerly of Neuralink
- Positioned as "high resolution without high risk" -- demonstrating that invasive surgery is not required for high-bandwidth BCI data

### INBRAIN Neuroelectronics

- European company manufacturing BCIs powered by **graphene**
- Received EUR 4 million grant from the Spanish Ministry of Industry and Tourism (May 2025) through the PERTE Chip initiative
- Part of Spain and the EU's push for semiconductor and microelectronics strategic autonomy

## Consumer and Commercial Applications

### Current Consumer BCI Products

The consumer BCI market is dominated by **non-invasive EEG headsets**, which are far less capable than clinical-grade implantable devices but accessible to the general public:

- **Muse (InteraXon):** Consumer-friendly EEG headband primarily used for meditation, mental fitness, and sleep monitoring. The Muse S (Gen 2) remains a popular option
- **Emotiv:** Offers higher-fidelity EEG devices for both consumers and researchers, including the Insight and EPOC models. Published a "BCI Headset Guide for 2026" in January 2026
- **OpenBCI Galea:** Open-source platform combining EEG with eye tracking, EMG, and other biosensors
- **40 Years of Zen:** Premium EEG-based neurofeedback device

- **Art creation:** Neuralink participant Audrey creating abstract art through thought-controlled cursor

## Ethical and Regulatory Landscape

### UNESCO Global Ethical Framework (November 2025)

The most significant regulatory development of 2025 was **UNESCO's adoption of the first global normative framework on the ethics of neurotechnology** on November 12, 2025:

- Adopted by all 194 UNESCO Member States at the General Conference in Samarkand, Uzbekistan
- Establishes the **"inviolability of the human mind"** as a core principle
- Key provisions:
  - Safeguards for mental privacy and human dignity
  - Warnings against use of neurotechnology in the workplace to monitor productivity or create data profiles on employees
  - Requirement for explicit consent and full transparency
  - Advisement against non-therapeutic use on children and young people whose brains are still developing
  - Regulation of products that may influence behavior or promote addiction
- Resulted from consultations beginning in 2021, with more than **8,000 contributions** from civil society, the private sector, academia, and member states
- Led by French scientist Herve Chneiweiss and American professor Nita Farahany
- UNESCO noted a **700% increase** in investment in neurotechnology companies between 2014 and 2021
- UNESCO is supporting 80+ countries in transposing these principles into national legislation

### Neurorights Movement

The concept of "neurorights" has gained significant traction:

- The **Neurorights Foundation** leads global efforts to ensure ethical use of neurotechnologies
- Key proposed neurorights include:
  - **Right to mental privacy:** Preventing neural activity from being deciphered without consent
  - **Right to mental identity:** Protecting the core sense of self
  - **Right to free will:** Ensuring decisions are not manipulated by neurotechnology
- Chile was the first country to enshrine neurorights in its constitution (2021)
- The EU's BCI regulation emphasizes the supremacy of human rights with legislation to protect users' rights
- Harvard's Petrie-Flom Center published analysis on the legal and ethical frontier of neurotech (February 2025)
- *The New York Times* published a major investigation: "Big Tech Wants Direct Access to Our Brains" (November 2025)
- UNICEF published a report on neurotechnology and children's rights (2025)

### Regulatory Challenges

- The **FDA's Breakthrough Device Designation** program is accelerating reviews but questions remain about long-term safety standards
- When BCIs fail, human trial participants have **few options** for recourse (Citeline report, January 2026)
- Consumer neurotechnology (EEG headbands, connected headphones) remains **largely unregulated** outside medical contexts
- Neural data can reveal thoughts, emotions, and reactions -- may be shared without consent through consumer devices

## Academic Research Frontiers

### Key Research Directions

1. **Speech Decoding:** Multiple groups achieving near-real-time thought-to-speech translation. Neuralink participants typing at 40 WPM; Paradromics targeting synthesized speech
2. **Motor Restoration:** Moving beyond cursor control to real-world three-dimensional actions (wheelchairs, robotic arms)
3. **Sensory Feedback:** Restored touch and proprioception -- conference at Society for Neuroscience (November 2025) highlighted "a new era of brain-computer interfaces: restored touch, accurate speech, seamless movement"
4. **Bidirectional BCIs:** Systems that both read from and write to the brain -- BISC platform offers both recording and stimulation
5. **AI-Powered Decoding:** Deep learning and large language models being applied to neural signal interpretation. Synchron's Chiral model; Merge Labs leveraging AI for ultrasound-based decoding
6. **Flexible/Soft Electrodes:** NeuroWorm and Chinese CAS soft electrodes addressing long-term biocompatibility
7. **Sonogenetics:** Merge Labs exploring genetic engineering to make cells more responsive to ultrasound
8. **Mental Health Applications:** Companies beginning to target depression, epilepsy, addiction, and eating disorders with neurostimulation-based BCIs

### Key Academic Institutions

- **Columbia University:** BISC platform (Ken Shepard lab)
- **Stanford University:** Neural decoding and AI models for BCI (Andreas Tolias, Byers Eye Institute)
- **University of Pennsylvania:** Neurosurgery and BCI systems (Bijan Pesaran)
- **Caltech:** Forest Neurotech/Merge Labs ultrasound BCI research (Mikhail Shapiro)
- **UC Davis:** BrainGate2 project
- **Chinese Academy of Sciences:** Invasive BCI and soft electrode research
- **RIKEN (Japan):** Partially invasive BCI for stroke rehabilitation

## Geopolitical Competition: The US-China BCI Race

### United States

- Dominates in number of companies, clinical trials, and FDA pathway
- Neuralink, Synchron, Paradromics, Precision Neuroscience, Merge Labs all US-based or US-headquartered
- Strong academic ecosystem (Stanford, Columbia, Caltech, Penn, UC Davis)
- FDA Breakthrough Device program enabling rapid clinical translation

### China

China has emerged as the most significant competitor to the US in BCI:

- **Government designation:** BCI officially labeled one of six **"industries of the future"** in the 15th Five-Year Plan
- **Timeline:** Integration into healthcare and manufacturing by **2027**; globally dominant industrial ecosystem by **2030**
- **Explosion of startups** entering the BCI space
- **Key achievements:**
  - CAS invasive BCI enabling real-world wheelchair control and robotic commands
  - NeuroWorm soft dynamic electrode (published in *Nature*)
  - First in-orbit verification of wireless BCI in space (February 2026)
  - Two-way BCI demonstrated
- **Institutional backing:** Chinese Academy of Sciences, Fudan University/Huashan Hospital, Northwestern Polytechnical University, Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology, Donghua University

### Other Regions

- **Japan:** RIKEN institute breakthroughs in stroke rehabilitation BCIs; government funding
- **Europe:** INBRAIN (Spain) with graphene-based BCIs; EU BCI regulations emphasizing human rights
- **Australia:** Synchron originated in Australia before relocating headquarters to New York
- **Canada/UK:** Neuralink expanding clinical trials internationally

## Outlook and Challenges

### Near-Term Trajectory (2026-2027)

- Neuralink targeting 20-30 additional implants and beginning high-volume production
- Paradromics first long-term implants in early 2026
- Synchron pivotal trial expected to begin
- Merge Labs developing ultrasound prototypes
- BISC platform moving toward expanded human studies
- China likely to scale clinical trials significantly

### Key Challenges

1. **Long-term safety:** Electrode degradation, immune response, and device longevity remain open questions. The first Neuralink patient experienced partial electrode detachment requiring software fixes
2. **Participant welfare:** When BCIs fail, participants have few options. The technology creates deep dependency -- removing a device could leave patients worse off than before
3. **Regulatory frameworks:** Most regulation applies only to medical BCIs; consumer neurotechnology remains a regulatory gap
4. **Cost and accessibility:** Current BCIs require expensive surgery and support infrastructure; widespread access remains years away
5. **Data privacy:** Neural data is extraordinarily sensitive; current legal frameworks are inadequate
6. **Scalability:** Moving from single-digit clinical participants to thousands of users requires manufacturing, surgical, and support infrastructure breakthroughs
7. **Ethical boundaries:** Questions about cognitive enhancement, mental surveillance, and the boundary between therapy and augmentation remain unresolved

### The Path to Commercialization

Clinical Trials Arena (August 2025) estimated that BCIs **could be commercialized by 2030**, based on patient demonstrations, ongoing clinical trials, and heavy investment. The Sparkco AI analysis (November 2025) noted that full PMA (premarket approval) for Neuralink is targeted for 2025-2026. The trajectory suggests that the first commercially available implantable BCIs for specific medical conditions could arrive within the next 2-4 years, with broader consumer applications further out.

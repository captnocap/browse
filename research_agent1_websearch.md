## Executive Summary

Brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) have entered a pivotal era in 2026. The field has transitioned from proof-of-concept laboratory demonstrations to multi-patient clinical trials, early commercial deployments, and high-volume manufacturing plans. At least four companies -- Neuralink, Synchron, Paradromics, and Precision Neuroscience -- are pursuing FDA-regulated clinical pathways, while ONWARD Medical is bridging BCI and spinal cord stimulation to restore movement. Non-invasive consumer devices continue to grow, AI-driven neural decoding has achieved near-conversational speech rates, and legislative bodies worldwide are scrambling to establish neurorights protections. The global BCI market, valued at approximately $2.94 billion in 2025, is projected to reach $13.86 billion by 2035.

## 1. Latest Scientific Breakthroughs

### BISC: A New Generation of Brain Chips

The most significant hardware breakthrough announced in late 2025 is the **Biological Interface System to Cortex (BISC)**, developed collaboratively by Columbia University, NewYork-Presbyterian Hospital, Stanford University, and the University of Pennsylvania. The entire implant is a single CMOS integrated circuit chip thinned to just 50 micrometers, with a total volume of approximately 3 mm cubed. It integrates:

- **65,536 electrodes**
- **1,024 simultaneous recording channels**
- **16,384 stimulation channels**

Its external relay station communicates via a custom ultrawideband radio link achieving **100 Mbps data bandwidth** -- at least 100 times higher throughput than any other wireless BCI currently available. BISC was developed under DARPA's Neural Engineering System Design program, and the Columbia and Stanford teams have launched a spin-off company, **Kampto Neurotech**, to commercialize the technology.

### Real-Time Speech Neuroprosthesis

A landmark paper published in *Nature Neuroscience* in March 2025 by a team led by Dr. Edward F. Chang (UCSF) and Dr. Gopala Anumanchipalli (UC Berkeley) solved the long-standing latency problem in speech neuroprostheses. Their streaming method synthesizes brain signals into audible speech in **near-real time** -- with a delay of just one-fortieth of a second, comparable to the natural delay a person experiences hearing their own voice.

### Inner Speech Decoding

Stanford researchers demonstrated in August 2025 that "inner speech" -- the internal monologue -- evokes clear and robust patterns of activity in motor brain regions, raising hope that future BCIs could decode intended words without requiring any physical attempt to speak.

### Speech Speed Records

- **Stanford (2023):** 62 words per minute (WPM), 3.4x faster than the previous record
- **UCSF (2023):** 78 WPM, more than 4x faster than prior records
- **UC Davis (2025):** Achieved real-time voice synthesis with 1/40th-of-a-second latency

A UC Davis patient used a BCI independently at home for more than two years without daily recalibration, achieving **99% accuracy** at outputting intended words.

### AI and Machine Learning Integration

The convergence of deep learning with neural data has yielded remarkably accurate decoders. Speech BCIs now infer words from brain activity at **99% accuracy** with less than 0.25-second latency. Closed-loop systems using AI are enabling self-learning and dynamically adaptive decoding.

## 2. Major Companies and Their Progress

### Neuralink

**Patient count:** As of mid-2025, at least 7 participants were using the N1 Link device daily. By September 2025, Neuralink reported 12 people worldwide with severe paralysis had received the implants.

**2026 manufacturing plans:** Elon Musk announced that Neuralink will begin **high-volume production** of BCI devices in 2026 and move to a streamlined, **almost entirely automated surgical procedure**. Device threads will go through the dura without the need to remove it -- described as "a big deal."

**Blindsight (Vision Restoration):** Neuralink is preparing first human clinical trials of its **Blindsight** implant in 2026. The device uses an external camera that wirelessly transmits visual data to a chip implanted in the visual cortex, designed to generate perception of vision even in people born blind. The device received FDA **Breakthrough Device Designation**, though experts caution that initial resolution will be low. The FDA emphasized that the designation does not mean Blindsight is yet considered safe or effective.

**Thread retraction complications:** In the first patient (Noland Arbaugh, implanted January 2024), approximately 85% of the implant's electrode threads retracted from the brain in the weeks following surgery, significantly reducing performance. Neuralink modified its algorithm to be more sensitive to neural population signals, reportedly resolving the issue. The patient continues to use the device.

**Funding:** Neuralink raised **$650 million** in a June 2025 funding round.

**Regulatory controversy:** In December 2025, Neuralink hired **David McMullen**, the former FDA director of the Office of Neurological and Physical Medicine Devices, to lead its medical affairs. This has raised questions about potential **regulatory capture**. Separately, an FDA official with oversight of Neuralink devices was fired amid broader HHS staffing changes in early 2025.

### Synchron

**COMMAND Trial results:** Synchron's COMMAND study -- the first FDA-approved IDE trial of a permanently implanted BCI -- enrolled six patients with severe chronic bilateral upper-limb paralysis. All six met the **primary safety endpoint** of no device-related serious adverse events resulting in death or permanent increased disability during the one-year post-implantation evaluation.

**Pivotal trial pathway:** Synchron is in ongoing conversations with the FDA about endpoints for a **pivotal trial** that could lead to the first premarket approval (PMA) of a BCI implant. Such a trial would take approximately two years to run before submission.

**Apple and Nvidia integration:** Synchron will be the **first BCI company to achieve native integration with Apple devices** (iPhone, iPad, Apple Vision Pro) through Apple's new BCI Human Interface Device (BCI HID) profile. A patient with ALS demonstrated thought-controlled home devices (lighting, music, robotic vacuum, pet feeder) using the Synchron Stentrode paired with Nvidia AI and Apple Vision Pro.

**Chiral AI model:** Synchron unveiled **Chiral**, described as the world's first cognitive AI brain foundation model, developed in collaboration with Nvidia. Built on the Nvidia Holoscan platform, it enables real-time neural decoding with minimal latency.

### Paradromics

**FDA approval for clinical trial:** In November 2025, Paradromics received an FDA IDE to evaluate its **Connexus BCI** for speech restoration, with the clinical study planned for Q1 2026. The trial will implant the device in two volunteers who lost the ability to speak due to neurological diseases and injuries.

**First-in-human recording completed:** Paradromics completed its first-in-human neural recording with the Connexus system.

**Technology:** Connexus uses over **65,000 electrodes** distributed across a flexible array that conforms to the brain's surface, enabling recording from a much larger area than traditional electrode arrays.

### Precision Neuroscience

**FDA 510(k) clearance (April 2025):** The **Layer 7 Cortical Interface** received the first full regulatory clearance granted to a next-generation wireless BCI company, for recording, monitoring, and stimulation of cortical brain activity with implantation durations up to 30 days.

**Device specs:** The Layer 7 features **1,024 electrodes** embedded in a flexible film one-fifth the thickness of a human hair.

**Medtronic partnership (January 2026):** Precision Neuroscience partnered with Medtronic to integrate the Layer 7 cortical interface with Medtronic's **StealthStation** surgical navigation system, aiming to accelerate widespread clinical adoption.

### ONWARD Medical

**ARC-BCI system:** As of January 2026, **seven patients** have received the investigational ARC-BCI Therapy, which pairs a cortical brain implant with spinal cord stimulation to restore thought-driven movement. The system uses AI to decode brain signals associated with movement intention and wirelessly transmits instructions to an implanted neurostimulator that stimulates targeted spinal cord regions.

**FDA recognition:** The ARC-BCI System received FDA **Breakthrough Device Designation** in 2024 and is included in the FDA's Total Product Life Cycle Advisory Program.

### Blackrock Neurotech

**Utah Array:** With up to 128 silicon penetrating microelectrodes per array (up to 1,024 when arrays are paired), the Utah Array has FDA clearance for monitoring brain electrical activity for up to 30 days. Blackrock is pursuing clearance for **long-term implantation**.

**Partnerships:** Blackrock partnered with Cognixion to distribute the **Axon-R**, a non-invasive AR-enabled BCI headset launched in May 2025.

**Future tech:** Blackrock is developing **Neuralace**, a flexible lattice technology for less invasive cortical coverage.

## 4. Consumer and Commercial Applications

### Non-Invasive Consumer Devices

The non-invasive BCI segment dominates the market, capturing **nearly 75% of total BCI market share** by 2025. Key consumer devices and companies include:

- **Emotiv:** Offers the Insight (5-channel EEG headset) and EPOC X (14-channel research-grade headset). In 2024, Emotiv introduced MN8 EEG earbuds, bringing brain-metrics to a true-wireless form factor.
- **Kernel:** Released the Flow 2 (2023), a wearable full-head-coverage TD-fNIRS system delivering fMRI-like spatial resolution that sets up in under three minutes.
- **NextMind:** Acquired by Snap Inc., integrating BCI into the AR/social media ecosystem.

### Consumer Applications

Current non-invasive consumer BCI applications include:
- **Neurofeedback training** for meditation and focus
- **Cognitive performance monitoring** for workplace productivity
- **Gaming and entertainment** control
- **Sleep quality optimization**
- **Wellness and mental health** tracking

### Emerging Integrations

The trend toward integration with AR/VR headsets and exoskeletons is expected to provide richer user experiences, with cloud-based decoding solutions complementing hardware headsets.

## 5. Ethical and Regulatory Landscape

### Neurorights Legislation

The global response to BCI's emergence has produced a patchwork of neural data protection laws:

**United States (State Level):**
- **California:** SB 1223 amended the CCPA to classify neural data as "sensitive personal information"
- **Colorado:** Enacted neural data protections under its state privacy law
- **Minnesota:** Enacted legislation with civil and criminal penalties for violations of neural data rights (signed May 2024)
- **Montana:** Has neural data laws on the books
- **Connecticut:** SB 1295 (effective July 1, 2026) will amend the Connecticut Data Privacy Act to include "neural data" as sensitive data
- **Pending legislation:** Illinois, Massachusetts, Vermont, Delaware

**United States (Federal Level):**
- The **MIND Act** (Management of Individuals' Neural Data Act of 2025) was proposed by Senators Cantwell, Schumer, and Markey, directing the FTC to study neural data collection, use, and processing

**International:**
- **Chile:** First country to legislate on neurotechnologies and include "brain rights" in its constitution
- **Brazil:** Rio Grande do Sul enacted neurorights protections
- **Mexico:** Advancing a constitutional amendment on neurorights
- **Uruguay:** Has a neurorights bill pending
- **UNESCO:** Appointed an expert group to prepare a global standard on ethics of neurotechnology (planned for adoption November 2025)
- **OECD:** Issued Recommendation on Responsible Innovation in Neurotechnology (2019, updated 2023)
- **China:** New BCI medical device standard (YY/T 1987-2025) implemented January 1, 2026

### Industry Practices

A 2024 Neurorights Foundation audit of 30 consumer neurotechnology companies found alarming results:
- **96.7%** of companies reserve the right to transfer brain data to third parties
- Nearly every company reviewed appeared to have access to consumer neural data with no meaningful limitations

### Cybersecurity Concerns

BCI cybersecurity is an active area of concern:
- Hackers can theoretically intercept and manipulate brain signals or gain direct control over connected devices
- Demonstrated attacks have forced virtual keyboard spellers to type undesired characters, caused brain-controlled drones to crash, and made neurofeedback interfaces report false states
- A Yale-led study (2025) recommends mandating non-surgical methods for updating and recovering devices, and strong authentication schemes for BCI software modifications
- Without effective guardrails, a widespread security breach in standardized BCI systems could affect millions of users simultaneously

### Key Ethical Issues

- **Cognitive liberty:** The right to mental self-determination
- **Mental privacy:** Protection against unauthorized access to neural data
- **Psychological continuity:** Ensuring BCI use does not alter personal identity
- **Equitable access:** Preventing BCI from becoming a tool of social stratification
- **Dual use:** Military and defense applications raising questions about coercion and autonomy
- **Premature commercialization:** Risk of outpacing neuroscientific understanding and ethical frameworks

## 6. Academic Research Frontiers

### Brain-to-Voice Neuroprosthesis
UC Berkeley and UCSF developed a streaming approach that synthesizes brain signals into audible speech in near-real time, published in *Nature Neuroscience* (March 2025).

### Long-Term Stability
A UC Davis patient used a BCI independently at home for over two years without daily recalibration, with 99% word accuracy -- demonstrating critical reliability for real-world deployment.

### Brain-to-Text Competition
UC Davis's Neuroprosthetics Lab (part of the BrainGate consortium) hosts the annual **Brain-to-Text** competition, an open-source public contest pushing machine learning innovation in neural speech decoding.

### Closed-Loop Neurorehabilitation
A systematic review published in JMIR Biomedical Engineering (2025) documents advancing closed-loop BCI systems integrating AI and ML for neurorehabilitation, enabling adaptive stimulation based on real-time brain state.

### Brain-Spine Digital Interfaces
Research led by EPFL (Switzerland) and continued by ONWARD Medical explores digital bridges between brain and spinal cord, using BCI to decode movement intention and epidural stimulation to activate spinal circuits.

### Depression and Mental Health BCIs
- The largest mental health BCI trial (400 participants, Shanghai Mental Health Center) tests a non-invasive BCI system for depression and bipolar disorder
- Georgia Tech researchers developed an AI model that identifies signs of depression relapse **five weeks before symptoms appear**
- Mount Sinai performed the first deep brain stimulation implant for depression in a clinical trial (March 2025)
- A November 2025 *Nature Communications* study showed that half of a small group receiving brain implants experienced significant improvement in depression symptoms

### Non-Invasive Frontier
Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory is developing new approaches to non-invasive BCI that aim to rival the signal quality of invasive systems, potentially expanding BCI access without surgical risk.

## 9. Defense and Military Applications

### DARPA Programs

- **N3 (Next-Generation Nonsurgical Neurotechnology):** Aims to develop high-performance, bi-directional brain-machine interfaces for able-bodied service members without requiring surgery. Applications include control of unmanned aerial vehicles, active cyber defense, and human-computer teaming during complex military missions.
- **Neural Engineering System Design (NESD):** Funded the development of the BISC chip (Columbia/Stanford), pushing the boundaries of high-bandwidth neural recording and stimulation.

### Military Applications Under Investigation

- Enhanced soldier cognitive performance
- Brain-controlled unmanned systems
- Real-time cognitive monitoring for fatigue and stress
- Accelerated training through neurostimulation

A 2025 paper in *The RUSI Journal* examined the risks of changing the human condition through soldier enhancement via BCIs, highlighting concerns about coercion, autonomy, and the ethics of military neurotechnology.

10. **Cybersecurity urgency:** As BCIs scale, the need for robust neural data security frameworks becomes critical
